A lattice gas is a fluid made of particles that hop between the points of a hexagonal grid and collide by a fixed rule, an idea of Frisch, Hasslacher and Pomeau from 1986. Nothing else is programmed: the flow around the plate and the vortex street behind it emerge from those collisions alone. Ternary means that each link holds -1, 0 or +1 relative to a local reference, which makes the gas less noisy and lets it reach a higher Reynolds number on the same grid. Black and white are the two directions of rotation, on a grey background; the further from grey, the stronger it is.

Method: FHP lattice gas (Frisch, Hasslacher and Pomeau, 1986), flow past a plate after d'Humières and Lallemand (1987).
Engine: Rust, on WebGPU or WebAssembly. Source: jacobi2000.
